Crypto Future Trading

Leveraged perpetual futures — traders go long or short without owning the underlying asset, with continuous PnL, margin, automatic liquidation and funding fees.

Overview

Futures trading lets a trader speculate on whether a coin's price will rise or fall without ever holding it. They open a long to profit from an increase or a short to profit from a decrease, and use leverage to control a larger position than their capital would otherwise allow.

As the market moves, unrealized profit and loss is recalculated continuously. If losses eat through the available margin, the position is liquidated automatically. Traders can close at any time to realize the result, and the platform charges trading fees plus a periodic funding fee exchanged between longs and shorts.

I built and maintained the backend behind all of that — the position engine, the margin and liquidation logic, and the APIs the Next.js frontend and the mobile app consume.

Key Features

  • Long & short positions — open in either direction on any supported perpetual contract.
  • Leverage — configurable multipliers, with margin requirements enforced at open and on every update.
  • Continuous PnL — unrealized profit/loss recalculated against the mark price as it moves.
  • Automatic liquidation — positions whose losses exceed available margin are closed by the engine, not by hand.
  • Funding fees — periodic funding settled between long and short holders on a fixed cycle.
  • Take-profit & stop-loss — pre-set exits that trigger without the trader being online.
  • Position & order history — full audit trail of every entry, exit, fee and liquidation.

What I Did

  • Built the position engine: opening, adjusting, partially closing and fully closing leveraged positions.
  • Implemented margin accounting and the liquidation trigger, so an underwater position is closed before it can go negative.
  • Implemented the funding-fee cycle that periodically settles value between long and short holders.
  • Designed and optimized the APIs used by the Next.js frontend and the mobile app.
  • Used Redis for mark-price and position-state reads that are hit on every tick.
  • Ran PnL, liquidation checks and funding settlement on queue workers so they don't block user requests.

Challenges & How I Solved Them

1

PnL that has to be right, continuously

Unrealized PnL changes on every price tick for every open position. Recomputing it lazily on read — and caching the inputs in Redis — kept the cost bounded instead of scaling with the number of open positions times the tick rate.

2

Liquidating in time

A liquidation that fires late can leave the account with negative equity. Margin is re-checked on every price update, and the liquidation path is kept short so it can execute before the position goes underwater.

3

Money arithmetic

Leverage multiplies rounding errors. Financial values are handled with precise decimal arithmetic rather than floats, so fees, PnL and margin all reconcile exactly.

4

Funding on a strict schedule

Funding must be applied once per cycle to every open position — no more, no less. Settlement is idempotent and keyed per position per cycle, so a retried job can't charge a trader twice.
